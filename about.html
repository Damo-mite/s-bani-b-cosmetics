A short history of shea butter

Shea butter is the fat pressed from the nut of the shea tree, Vitellaria paradoxa. The tree grows in a band of African savanna from Senegal across to Uganda, often called the shea belt. Ghana sits inside that belt.

For centuries, communities have cracked, roasted, milled, and kneaded the nuts by hand. A great deal of that work is still done by women, and shea remains an important income for many households. The butter has long been used on skin and hair, and also in cooking.

Unrefined shea keeps more of its natural ivory-to-gold colour and a faint nutty scent. Refined shea is paler and milder. S'Bani B’s raw tubs stay on the unrefined side, then get whipped so the butter spreads more easily.
